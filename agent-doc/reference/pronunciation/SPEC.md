# Pronunciation requirements

Single-word pronunciation first requests audio for the exact selected word from the public Youdao Dictionary voice endpoint, choosing the UK or US variant for the preferred accent. Exactly one plain English word leaves the device: letters plus apostrophes or hyphens, at most 48 characters. Phrases, sentences, examples, chapter titles, surrounding book text and model credentials are never sent to it. The endpoint is public but undocumented and unendorsed; it is called without a key, account, service agreement or permission, so coverage, availability and audio quality are not guaranteed. Its bytes are provider-supplied, not a verified human recording or freely licensed asset, and Peekturn claims no licence over them. Audio is accepted only when it looks like MPEG audio and remains bounded and memory-only. Redirects are refused. The source credit appears in Settings, not the reading popup; notices record the service and absence of upstream permission.

Settings → Pronunciation owns one persisted sentence-speech provider choice shared by continuous narration, selected phrases, generated examples and the sentence preview: Microsoft Read Aloud (default), Android system TTS or Baidu English. Missing or invalid saved choices use Microsoft. These actions use only the selected provider; unavailable or invalid audio and playback failures produce a visible error, never an automatic provider change. Reading surfaces show notices and failures, not provider selectors or system-voice configuration actions. Changing provider stops existing speech and narration; the next request uses the new choice. Single-word pronunciation remains Youdao first, then Microsoft, Baidu and installed system speech if necessary, independently of this setting.

Microsoft and Baidu are public, key-free, unofficial services with no availability, quality or licence guarantee. Microsoft requests the preferred US or UK English voice. Baidu has one English voice and displays a notice that the requested accent is not guaranteed. Online popup requests carry only the exact requested text, never surrounding context, chapter titles or model credentials; text beyond the client limit fails rather than switching providers. Popup clips remain memory-only. Android system TTS prefers an installed English voice, offline before network and accent as a preference rather than a gate. When no explicit English voice can be selected but the engine accepts an English language request, synthesis may proceed even if the current voice metadata is absent or names another language, with an explicit warning that its language or accent cannot be confirmed. This is permission to attempt speech, not proof of English pronunciation or installed resources. Explicit unsupported or missing-data responses and not-installed voice flags still fail; actual synthesis and playback errors remain explicit. Single-word system fallback retains its existing final unprobed-device-voice pass; an explicitly chosen Android request does not use that pass. No microphone permission.

Settings → Pronunciation shows the provider choices, one US/UK accent choice and adjacent compact word and sentence previews. Provider selection is available only here. The expandable audio details retain service disclosure and the system-speech settings entry point. Playback errors, provider limitation notices and current audio credit stay visible without expanding details. The lookup popup omits audio credit and configuration actions while retaining speaker, stop, notices, diagnostic reports and failure dismissal. The word preview exercises the unchanged word route; the sentence preview exercises only the selected provider.

Manual term speaker is available before AI completes. Playback toggles Stop; a new utterance replaces rather than queues. Auto-pronounce is global, off by default, once per new lookup across Explain/Dictionary. Switching tabs or retrying does not repeat it. Stop on close, adjust, leaving reader, or background. Late initialization/callbacks cannot play obsolete text. Release resources and respect audio focus/media routing.

Continuous read-aloud retains its separate foreground-service and Readium TTS navigator lifetime while consuming the same provider setting. Microsoft and Baidu complete sentence clips are cached privately under provider/voice-specific keys for offline replay; Android system speech bypasses that audio cache. No narration route switches provider automatically. Android pause/resume restarts the current sentence because platform speech has no seekable audio position.

Settings → Developer logs exposes a scrollable, selectable report and a pinned one-tap Copy action. Each opening and copy exports current safe device/app/OS, installed/default TTS engine packages and versions, recent own-app exit reasons where Android supports them, and persisted feature/crash events. Speech events include bounded per-engine voice metadata, selection/language API returns and actual synthesis callbacks, never the spoken text. Diagnostics stay in no-backup app-private storage, rotate at 256 KiB with an explicit marker, and survive restart. Reading surfaces retain concise notices/errors; full narration diagnostics belong in Settings.
