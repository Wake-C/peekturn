# Pronunciation requirements

Single-word pronunciation first requests audio for the exact selected word from the public Youdao Dictionary voice endpoint, choosing the UK or US variant for the preferred accent. Exactly one plain English word leaves the device — letters plus apostrophes or hyphens, at most 48 characters — and nothing else: selected phrases, sentences, examples, book text, chapter titles, selection context and model credentials are never sent to it. The endpoint is public but undocumented and unendorsed; it is called without a key, account, service agreement or permission, so coverage, availability and audio quality are not guaranteed and may change without notice. Its bytes are provider-supplied rather than a verified human or freely licensed recording, and Margin claims no licence over them. Audio arrives as a bounded in-memory response, is accepted only when it actually looks like MPEG audio, and is never persisted, cached or bundled. Redirects are refused rather than followed, so a word is never resent to another host. The app credits the source by name and link wherever that audio plays, in the reading popup and in Settings, and claims nothing further, because the service states no creator or licence. The notices record the service and that no upstream permission was acquired.

Text that is not a single word — a selected phrase or a generated example — is spoken by a second public, key-free speech endpoint (Google Translate's `translate_tts`), requested with the US or UK locale the reader prefers. It is unofficial and undocumented: no licence, availability or quality guarantee is claimed, no support commitment or endorsement by the service is implied, and it may change or disappear. Only the exact text the reader asked to hear leaves the device; surrounding context, book text, chapter titles and model credentials never do. As with word audio, it is credited by name and link wherever it plays, is never persisted or cached, and carries no rights claim from Margin. A request is made only for text of at most roughly 190 characters, so a longer passage is never attempted online, even though the endpoint has answered a 199-character sentence. Answers were observed in about half a second for a ten-word sentence and for text with punctuation and quotation marks, six rapid requests were all answered, and the US and UK requests returned different audio; those are observations, not a guarantee of availability, coverage or speech quality beyond what was measured. A word whose dictionary recording is unavailable also tries this endpoint before system speech, so the word path is dictionary audio, then the speech endpoint, then installed English speech, while phrases and examples are the speech endpoint and then installed English speech. The notices record this endpoint, its unofficial status and the absence of any permission or licence.

If no usable audio is available, speak the exact selected term with an installed English system voice. Phrases and complete examples never go to the word-audio dictionary endpoint, which is not a sentence or general text-to-speech service. System speech is the fallback for every path: the service being unavailable or redirecting, an answer that is oversized, malformed or unexpected, and text beyond the online length limit all end in installed English speech. Prefer usable offline English, requested accent when available; another installed English accent or generic English is a valid fallback with a nonblocking notice. A country mismatch alone is not an installation error. Never select a non-English voice. Try standard language selection when enumeration is empty or rejects candidates. Distinguish genuinely missing English data, unavailable engine and playback failure. Refresh capabilities on retry after system settings. No microphone permission.

Settings → Pronunciation shows one auto-pronounce toggle, one US/UK accent choice and adjacent compact word and sentence previews. The explanation of the online audio routes and the system-speech entry point stay behind an expandable details control, but playback errors, fallback notices and the current audio credit stay visible without expanding it. That explanation states the same facts without overclaiming: both online routes are free, key-free and unofficial, neither is guaranteed to answer, only the text the reader asked to hear leaves the device, and system speech is the fallback. The previews speak the real paths: the word preview tries recorded word audio, then the speech endpoint, then system speech; the sentence preview uses the speech endpoint with system speech as fallback.

Manual term speaker is available before AI completes. Playback toggles Stop; a new utterance replaces rather than queues. Auto-pronounce is global, off by default, once per new lookup across Explain/Dictionary. Switching tabs or retrying does not repeat it. Stop on close, adjust, leaving reader, or background. Late initialization/callbacks cannot play obsolete text. Release resources and respect audio focus/media routing.
