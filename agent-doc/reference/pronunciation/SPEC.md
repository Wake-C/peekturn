# Pronunciation requirements

Single-word pronunciation first requests audio for the exact selected word from the public Youdao Dictionary voice endpoint, choosing the UK or US variant for the preferred accent. Exactly one plain English word leaves the device — letters plus apostrophes or hyphens, at most 48 characters — and nothing else: selected phrases, sentences, examples, book text, chapter titles, selection context and model credentials are never sent to it. The endpoint is public but undocumented and unendorsed; it is called without a key, account, service agreement or permission, so coverage, availability and audio quality are not guaranteed and may change without notice. Its bytes are provider-supplied rather than a verified human or freely licensed recording, and Margin claims no licence over them. Audio arrives as a bounded in-memory response, is accepted only when it actually looks like MPEG audio, and is never persisted, cached or bundled. Redirects are refused rather than followed, so a word is never resent to another host. The app credits the source by name and link wherever that audio plays, in the reading popup and in Settings, and claims nothing further, because the service states no creator or licence. The notices record the service and that no upstream permission was acquired.

Text that is not a single word — a selected phrase or a generated example — is spoken by a public, key-free speech service. The first is Microsoft's read-aloud service, the one Microsoft Edge uses for its read-aloud voices: it needs no key or account, the app computes the request token locally, and the US or UK English voice the reader prefers is requested there. It is undocumented and unendorsed: no licence, availability or quality guarantee is claimed, no support commitment or endorsement by Microsoft is implied, and it may change or disappear. When it returns no usable audio the same text is retried once against a second key-free fallback, Baidu Translate's speech endpoint. Baidu offers a single English voice, so it cannot honour the US/UK preference: on that route the accent is a substitution, and the clip the app plays carries its own nonblocking notice that the requested accent is not guaranteed rather than claiming it was met. Only the exact text the reader asked to hear leaves the device; surrounding context, book text, chapter titles and model credentials never do. As with word audio, whichever service supplied the audio is credited by name and link wherever it plays, is never persisted or cached, and carries no rights claim from Margin. A request is made only for text of at most roughly 190 characters, so a longer passage is never attempted online. The Microsoft host answered its voice list (HTTP 200, 171 KB) from both a desktop network and an Android 15 emulator on which Google's speech host was unreachable (100% packet loss), and both the Microsoft and the Baidu services returned MPEG audio there; speech quality beyond those samples is unverified. The earlier Google Translate speech route was removed because it could not be reached from the user's network. A word whose dictionary recording is unavailable tries the Microsoft service, then Baidu, before system speech, so the word path is dictionary audio, then Microsoft, then Baidu, then installed English speech, while phrases and examples are Microsoft, then Baidu, then installed English speech. The notices record both services, their unofficial status and the absence of any permission or licence.

If no usable audio is available, speak the exact selected term with an installed English system voice. Phrases and complete examples never go to the word-audio dictionary endpoint, which is not a sentence or general text-to-speech service. System speech is the fallback for every path: the online services being unavailable, an answer that is oversized, malformed or unexpected, and text beyond the online length limit all end in installed English speech once the online routes are exhausted. Prefer usable offline English, requested accent when available; another installed English accent or generic English is a valid fallback with a nonblocking notice. A country mismatch alone is not an installation error. Never select a non-English voice. Try installed English voices best-first, then standard language selection for the requested accent, the other English country and plain English. When none of those succeeds, the engine's own voice is the last device attempt, used without selecting any voice or language and only for text whose letters are all Latin script, always labelled with a notice that the requested English accent is not guaranteed. Every installed engine is tried for a real English voice before any substitution: the system default engine first, then other engines declaring Android's text-to-speech service, each with installed English voices best-first and then the language probes. Only once all of them have declined does that last attempt ask the engines that started, in the same order, for the voice they already hold; an engine that still cannot speak gives way to the next engine of that final pass before anything is reported. A failure states only what was observed and carries a copyable report of it, shown under the error in both the reading popup and the Settings previews and never on a successful request: one line per online route that actually ran, with the stage it reached and its elapsed milliseconds, then the device clock and app version, then the device-speech evidence. The device-speech outcomes stay distinct: the engine reports its English voice data missing, no engine offers an English voice, the engine never started, or an engine accepted the English text and then failed to speak it — a playback failure, never a missing voice; "not installed" is claimed only when an engine itself answers that its data is not installed yet. Text the online services cannot carry, such as a selection beyond their length limit, is reported as declined rather than as a failed route. Refresh capabilities on retry after system settings. No microphone permission.

Settings → Pronunciation shows one auto-pronounce toggle, one US/UK accent choice and adjacent compact word and sentence previews. The explanation of the online audio routes and the system-speech entry point stay behind an expandable details control, but playback errors, fallback notices and the current audio credit stay visible without expanding it. That explanation states the same facts without overclaiming: every online route is free, key-free and unofficial, none is guaranteed to answer, only the text the reader asked to hear leaves the device, and system speech is the fallback. The previews speak the real paths: the word preview tries recorded word audio, then the Microsoft service, then Baidu, then system speech; the sentence preview tries the Microsoft service, then Baidu, with system speech as the fallback.

Manual term speaker is available before AI completes. Playback toggles Stop; a new utterance replaces rather than queues. Auto-pronounce is global, off by default, once per new lookup across Explain/Dictionary. Switching tabs or retrying does not repeat it. Stop on close, adjust, leaving reader, or background. Late initialization/callbacks cannot play obsolete text. Release resources and respect audio focus/media routing.

These requirements govern lookup pronunciation. Continuous read-aloud is a separate path with its own requirements: it uses the Microsoft read-aloud service only, has no Baidu or system-speech fallback, and stores each complete sentence clip in app-private persistent cache for offline replay. It shares the speech client and voice choices, not this latest-only memory-only playback lifetime or the fallback chain above.
