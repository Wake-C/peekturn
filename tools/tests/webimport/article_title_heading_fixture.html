Notes From a Year of Reading Slowly

Synthetic Author · 6 min read

Speed was never the point. The useful change was noticing when a paragraph required a second pass, and then taking that second pass without treating it as a delay.

Slow reading is not a performance. It is a way of leaving enough room for disagreement: with the author, with an earlier version of the reader's own opinion, and with the easy summary that arrives before the argument has actually been understood.

Keeping a short record helped. Not a formal review, just a few lines about the claim that survived the reading and the claim that did not. Over a year those lines became a map of what the reader actually cared about.

The map was more useful than a list of finished books. It showed which subjects kept returning, which authors were read for comfort rather than challenge, and which questions were still open after several attempts to answer them.

None of this requires a specialized tool. A notebook, a bookmark, and the willingness to stop in the middle of a chapter are enough. The habit is the infrastructure.
