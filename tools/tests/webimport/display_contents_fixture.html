How I Turn Learning Into a Workflow in the AI Era

Tw93 @HiTw93 · 5 min read

Before AI, my way of learning was much more old school. I would read books, go through the blogs of well-known people in a field, and keep taking notes along the way. It was slow, but there was real joy in it. I still remember learning WebGL like that.

Now that AI is here, my taste has not really changed. I still dislike the fast-food style of learning online. I still prefer to choose good material and spend real time with it. Moving slower and truly understanding something beats rushing through summaries.

Collecting high-quality source material comes first. That usually includes strong papers from recent years, key blog posts from major model labs, posts and essays from people leading model work, courses from universities, and classic repositories that build a model from scratch. Then I automate downloading, converting, cleaning and organizing it.

After that, I start reading and filtering. Material I can understand directly, I read carefully. For harder pieces, I translate and read again. At this stage I am not trying to fully master everything; I want a working understanding of the field and its core technical logic. By the end of this round, half of the material is usually gone.

Once I have a rough map of the field, I outline the article: what to cover, which sources belong to which section, and what a reader should understand at the end. Writing for other people means knowing where they will get lost and what level of explanation they need.

Then comes the real labor. I write section by section, filling in explanations and making the whole thing complete. AI helps me refine what I already have: remove repetition, fix weak transitions and expose missing background. It is not writing the article for me.

When the draft is in decent shape, I read it again myself. AI stays a tool. Once you let it take over your judgment, the whole thing becomes less meaningful. I keep editing, adjusting and tuning, the way I test code. Then I publish it.
